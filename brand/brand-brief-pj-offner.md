# Creative brief — Mulligan Mints

**For:** PJ Offner
**From:** Johannes Le Roux, Mulligan Mints
**Date:** August 2026
**Deliverable:** Full brand identity, ready to apply to a printed tin, a display
box, and a global licensing conversation.

---

## 1. The one-line version

**Mulligan Mints. Like it never happened.**

A strong pressed mint, sold in a tin, for the shot you'd rather forget.

## 2. What we are actually making

A 35g tin of sugar-free pressed mints. Genuinely strong — the Fisherman's Friend
end of the spectrum, not the Mentos end. Three flavours at launch. Sold in golf
club pro shops and bars across South Africa, then taken global.

The tin is not packaging. **The tin is the product.** It lives in a golf bag, on
a bar counter, in a cup holder. It gets kept, refilled, handed around and
photographed. Design for the object, not for the shelf.

## 3. The idea

A mulligan is the shot you take again. The one that didn't count. Every golfer
knows it, nobody officially allows it, and everybody takes one.

That's the whole brand: **a small, forgiving second chance.** The mint is the
mulligan. You take one, and whatever just happened — the shank off the first tee,
the three-putt, the thing you said at the bar — is quietly wiped from the record.

It should feel like an in-joke that got printed properly. Confident enough not to
explain itself.

## 4. Design direction

### The reference

Think of the Irish cousin of a British heritage brand. A tin that looks like it
has been made the same way since 1890 — because a heritage tin is the most
credible object in confectionery — but drawn by someone who is clearly having fun.

- **Irish green.** Deep, saturated, slightly bottle-green. Not the bright green of
  a sports drink.
- **Clover.** Yes, but earn it — as a mark, an emblem, a piece of engraving. Not
  as a St Patrick's Day decoration.
- **Heritage tin conventions:** crests, scrollwork, medallions, ornamental
  borders, a founding date, an ornate wordmark, a "by appointment" register of
  language. The visual grammar of something old and slightly self-important.
- **The modern edge:** the illustration should be *good* — genuinely well drawn,
  with wit in the detail. This is the part that makes it work for an American
  golf-content audience rather than reading as a museum piece.

### The tension to hold

**Heritage on the outside, joke on the inside.** The tin should look like it
belongs in a 1920s chemist. The copy on it should make somebody laugh at the
counter. If the design is entirely serious it's a pastiche; if it's entirely
funny it's a novelty gift. It has to be both, at once, and confident about it.

The best comparison for the register: **Altoids.** Ornate Victorian tin,
completely straight-faced, and the words on it — *"The Original Celebrated
Curiously Strong Mints"* — are a joke told with a perfectly straight face. That's
the target.

### What we are not

- Not a sports or energy brand. No dynamism, no speed lines, no gradients.
- Not a wellness brand. No sans-serif minimalism, no muted pastels, no white space.
- Not a novelty gift brand. No cartoon golfers, no comic-sans wink.
- Not the Chinese supplier's reference artwork. The image in the brief pack is a
  **spec reference only** — tin dimensions and carton configuration. Ignore its design entirely.

## 5. The Irish thing — an important constraint

The Irish reference is central and it has a hard limit.

**The product is manufactured in China. The company is South African.** Under SA
labelling law (R146) a label must not mislead as to origin, and UK and EU rules
are stricter — which matters because those are markets we intend to license into.

So: **Mulligan is a character, a legend, a patron saint of the second chance —
never a provenance claim.** Concretely, this means:

- ❌ No "Product of Ireland", "Irish recipe", "Made in Ireland", tricolour, or
  harp.
- ❌ No shamrock-plus-Gaelic-lettering lockup that reads as a country-of-origin
  mark.
- ✅ A named figure with a mythology. Green, clover, and heritage tin conventions
  used as *style*, in a register that's obviously legend rather than label.
- ✅ Country of origin stated plainly and correctly wherever the regulations
  require it.

This costs us nothing creatively and removes a real risk to the licensing plan.

## 6. Deliverables

### Core identity
1. Primary wordmark, plus a secondary/stacked lockup
2. Brand mark or emblem that works standalone at 8mm (embossed on a tin lid,
   stamped on a ball marker, as a favicon)
3. Colour palette — primary, secondary, and a flavour-coding system for three
   SKUs plus room for a fourth and fifth
4. Typography — display face for the wordmark, plus text faces for pack copy
   and digital, all properly licensed for commercial and packaging use
5. Illustration style: one fully worked hero illustration plus enough supporting
   assets to extend the system

### Packaging
6. **Tin artwork** — lid, base and sides, for three flavours, to the supplier
   dieline (dimensions to follow with the samples)
7. **Display box artwork** — the 12-tin counter unit. This is the most important
   piece of retail collateral we have; it does the selling in a pro shop.
8. **Shipper carton** markings

### Applications
9. Pro-shop counter display and a golf-day / sampling kit
10. Social templates — this brand lives on Instagram and YouTube thumbnails
11. Merchandise starters: cap, tee bag, ball marker, towel
12. Digital: logo suite, favicon, basic web styling direction

### The book
13. **Brand guidelines** — the identity, tone of voice, do's and don'ts, and the
    story. This is not an afterthought. It is a core asset: it is what a global
    licensee is buying in year 4, and it is what stops the brand drifting once
    other people start applying it.

### Files
All working files (`.ai`, `.psd`, `.indd`) delivered to us with full transfer of
copyright. Exports of every deliverable in print-ready and screen formats.

## 7. Constraints

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Print** | Tin printing has real limits — offset on tinplate, limited spot colours, no fine gradients, and metal shows through. Design for tinplate from the first sketch, not as an adaptation. |
| **Small sizes** | The wordmark must survive at 8mm on a ball marker and at 16px as a favicon. |
| **Legibility from a metre** | The display box has to be readable across a pro shop counter. |
| **Regulatory space** | The label must carry ingredients, nutritional table, sweetener declarations, allergens, net weight, country of origin, importer details, batch and best-before. That is more real estate than it sounds. Leave room for it, and plan for a possible front-of-pack warning logo under draft R3337. |
| **Scalability** | Three flavours now, likely five within two years, and possibly a second format. The system has to extend without a redesign. |

## 9. How we will judge it

One question: **would somebody keep the empty tin?**

If yes, it works. Everything else — the shelf standout, the licensing value, the
social content, the reason a bar puts it on the counter — follows from that.

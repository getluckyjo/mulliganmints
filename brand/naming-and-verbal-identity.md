# Verbal identity

How Mulligan Mints sounds. Draft — to be finalised with PJ Offner's brand book.

---

## The name

**Mulligan Mints.**

It works because it does three things at once: it names the product category
plainly, it plants the flag in golf without a golf ball in the logo, and it
carries an Irish surname that gives the heritage design somewhere true to stand.

Alliterative, easy to say, and it survives translation as a proper noun — which
matters for licensing.

## The tagline

**Like it never happened.**

Deadpan. Doesn't explain the joke. Works on a tin, on a cap, on a thumbnail, and
as the last line of a piece of content.

**Keep it in the golf frame.** The shank, the three-putt, the drive out of
bounds, the thing you said on the 4th. It is about the shot you'd rather forget —
never about hiding what you have been drinking. That reading is available, it is
funny, and it will get the brand declined by a pharmacy buyer and flagged by a
licensee's brand safety review. Not worth it. See `ops/risk-register.md` R-07.

## Tone of voice

**Dry. Old-fashioned. Faintly ceremonial. Never explains itself.**

The voice of an institution that takes a very silly thing extremely seriously.
Language borrowed from clubhouse notices, apothecary labels and rulebooks —
applied to forgiveness for bad golf.

| We sound like | We don't sound like |
| --- | --- |
| A clubhouse notice board | A sports brand |
| A rulebook, subverted | A wellness brand |
| An apothecary label from 1890 | A meme account |
| A caddie who has seen everything | A challenger brand explaining why it's different |

### Rules

1. **Short.** If it needs a second sentence it isn't finished.
2. **Straight-faced.** The joke is in the formality, not in the punchline.
3. **Never wink.** No exclamation marks, no "see what we did there".
4. **Never explain the mulligan.** Anyone who plays golf knows. Anyone who
   doesn't will work it out or enjoy not knowing.
5. **Talk about strength honestly.** The mint really is strong. Say so plainly
   and let it be a warning rather than a boast.

## Lines to work from

*For the tin, the box, the merch and the content. PJ to select and refine — the
register matters more than any individual line.*

- Like it never happened.
- Strictly one per incident.
- No shot was played. No mint was eaten. Nothing occurred.
- For the shot that didn't count.
- Take another.
- Administered on the tee. Effective immediately.
- Not a mint. A second opinion.
- Contains no evidence.
- The committee has reviewed the footage. There was no footage.
- Play it as it lies. Or don't.
- Recommended for the fourth hole, the ninth, and the entire back nine.
- One tin. Eighteen second chances.

## Naming the range

Flavours should be named in the same register — not "Peppermint", "Spearmint",
"Berries". The strength is the story, so name for the *effect*, and let the
descriptor sit small underneath.

Working directions to explore:

| Direction | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Rules-of-golf language | **The Provisional**, **The Drop**, **Ground Under Repair** |
| Score language | **The Snowman**, **The Sandy**, **The Gimme** |
| Strength-as-warning | **Extra Strong**, **Uncommonly Strong**, **Strictly Strong** |

Recommendation: **rules-of-golf naming for the flavour, with the flavour
descriptor in small type underneath** — e.g. *The Provisional · Peppermint*. It's
funnier, more ownable, and it survives export better than a South African
in-joke would.

## What we never say

- Anything about masking alcohol, or about what you've been drinking.
- Any health, medicinal or functional claim. It is a sweet, not a lozenge — and
  a medicinal claim changes our regulatory category entirely (see
  `legal/compliance-checklist-sa.md`).
- Any claim to be Irish, Irish-made, or made to an Irish recipe.
- "Curiously strong" — that is Altoids' and it is trademarked.

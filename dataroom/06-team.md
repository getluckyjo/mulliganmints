## The Get Lucky question an investor will ask

**"Your plan leans on 30 courses and 600 promotions a year that belong to a
different company. What happens if that access goes away?"**

It is a fair question and it is already on the risk register as R-13. The honest
answer has three parts:

1. Get Lucky is Johannes's own company, so the access is not at the mercy of a
   third party's goodwill.
2. It is an accelerant, not the plan. The distributor's sales team is the route
   to market; Get Lucky makes the golf half of it start warm and gives us a
   sampling channel we would otherwise have to buy.
3. **It is not yet in writing, and it should be.** Gap 10 in the dataroom
   register. A one-page access agreement between Get Lucky Golf Club and the
   Mulligan Mints company — what access is granted, on what terms, for how long —
   costs nothing and closes the question before it is asked.

**Do that before the room opens.** An investor who asks this and gets "it's fine,
it's my company" has found a governance answer they did not want.

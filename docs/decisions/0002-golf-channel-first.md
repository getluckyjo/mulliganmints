# 0002 — Enter through golf clubs, not retail

**Date:** August 2026 · **Status:** Accepted

## Context

A new confectionery brand can enter through national retail, through the
on-consumption trade, or through a niche channel.

## Options

1. **Golf clubs first**, direct
2. **National retail first** (Checkers, Clicks, Dis-Chem)
3. **Bars and restaurants first**

## Decision

Golf clubs, direct, for at least the first 18 months. Retail is deliberately
blocked until month 19.

## Why

- **The buyer is one person.** A club pro decides in one conversation. No listing
  fee, no planogram, no range review.
- **300 serviceable clubs is winnable.** 2,500 bars and 1,400 retail doors are not,
  with one rep.
- **Retail is where challenger brands die early.** R1.4m of listing fees is
  modelled across the plan, plus 65-day terms and promotional grids. Spending
  that before we have sell-through data is how a pre-seed disappears.
- **Golf clubs are a marketing budget disguised as a sales channel.** Every piece
  of content and every proof point comes from there.

## Consequences

- Slower revenue in years 1–2 than a retail-first plan would show on paper.
- Break-even lands in month 21, not year 1.
- Golf stays direct permanently, even at scale — 300 outlets is worth the cost to
  serve. Bars move to distributors from year 2; golf never does.
- When we do approach a retail buyer, we arrive with sell-through data and an
  audience rather than a hope.

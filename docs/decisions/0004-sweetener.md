# 0004 — Which sweetener

**Date:** August 2026 · **Status:** OPEN — decision required before artwork

## Context

The product is sugar-free. Which sweetener is used is a regulatory, cost, taste
and brand decision at once — and it blocks both the formula and the tin artwork.

Draft regulation **R3337** proposes a mandatory front-of-pack warning logo for
products containing **artificial sweeteners**. R146 remains current law, but
R3337 is coming.

## Options

| | Pros | Cons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Xylitol** | Natural-derived, dental benefit, clean taste, no artificial-sweetener warning logo | More expensive; laxative threshold warning; **toxic to dogs**, which matters when the tin lives in a golf bag |
| **Sorbitol / isomalt** | Cheap, standard for pressed mints, good tablet integrity | Polyol laxative warning may apply |
| **Steviol glycosides** | Natural, no warning logo, clean label | Aftertaste that is hard to hide even behind strong mint |
| **Aspartame / ace-K** | Cheapest, technically simplest | **Almost certainly triggers the R3337 warning logo.** Consumer sentiment moving against. |

## Leaning

**Xylitol or a xylitol/isomalt blend**, subject to cost and to the samples
tasting right. Keeps the label clean, avoids the warning logo, and offers a
dental story we can use without making a medicinal claim.

## Blocked on

- Supplier cost impact per tin for each option
- Taste evaluation of the physical samples

## Consequences either way

- **Leave physical space in the tin artwork for a front-of-pack warning logo
  regardless.** Re-tooling tin artwork is expensive; leaving space is free.
- If xylitol is chosen, consider whether a "keep away from dogs" note is
  warranted given the on-course use case.

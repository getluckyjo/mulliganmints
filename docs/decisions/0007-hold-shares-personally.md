# 0007 — Hold the founders' shares personally, not through a trust

**Date:** August 2026 · **Status:** Accepted

## Context

The exit analysis raised how the founders should hold their shares. The question
matters now rather than later, because moving shares into a trust once they have
value is a disposal at market value — it triggers CGT on the way in and possibly
donations tax with it.

## The tax position

Effective capital gains tax on a share disposal:

| Structure | Inclusion × rate | Effective CGT |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| **Individual** | 40% × 45% | **18.0%** |
| Trust — gain vested in resident beneficiaries | conduit under s25B | 18.0% |
| Trust — gain retained in the trust | 80% × 45% | 36.0% |
| Company | 80% × 27% | 21.6%, then 20% dividends tax to extract ≈ 37% |

**A trust never beats holding personally on exit tax; at best it matches.** It
reaches 18% only through the conduit principle — the gain must be vested in
**resident** beneficiaries in the same year of assessment. Since 1 March 2025 the
flow-through applies to residents only, so a beneficiary who has emigrated pulls
the gain back into the trust at 36%.

Sources in `research/sources.md`.

## Decision

**Founders hold their shares personally.** No trust.

## Why

- It is the cheapest structure on exit — 18%, and nothing beats it.
- It is simple. No trust deed, no independent trustee, no annual trust returns,
  no risk of the structure being attacked as a sham if it is not properly
  administered.
- The conduit route to 18% depends on vesting correctly every year and on
  beneficiaries staying South African resident. That is an ongoing obligation
  and an ongoing risk, in exchange for no saving.

## What we are giving up

**Estate duty planning.** Shares held personally form part of the founder's
estate. At 20% estate duty above the R3.5m abatement (25% above R30m), a stake
worth R24m creates a material estate duty exposure that a trust would have
sheltered.

**Asset protection.** Personally held shares are exposed to personal creditors
in a way trust-held assets are not.

These are real losses. They are accepted because the founders want the simplest
possible structure at this stage, and because neither becomes urgent until the
business is demonstrably valuable — which it is not yet.

## Revisit when

- **The business is clearly working and worth something material.** At that point
  compare the CGT and donations tax cost of moving shares into a trust against
  the estate duty saved. The comparison gets worse every year the value grows, so
  if it is ever going to happen, earlier is cheaper.
- **A founder's personal circumstances change** — marriage, emigration, a
  creditor risk from another venture.
- **Before signing anything at exit.** Get the structure reviewed once there is a
  real buyer and a real number, not before.

*General information, not tax advice. Confirm with a tax adviser.*

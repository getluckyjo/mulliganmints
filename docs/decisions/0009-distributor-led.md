# 0009 — Distributor-led from day 1, golf and bars

**Date:** August 2026 · **Status:** Accepted
**Changes the route to market in** [0002](0002-golf-channel-first.md) and
[0008](0008-bootstrap-route.md)

## Context

The plan assumed direct selling by the two founders, with a distributor taking
the bar tail from year 3. The field coverage arithmetic in
`gtm/sales-and-distribution-strategy.md` showed that does not work: on a
four-week call cycle the plan demanded **53 calls a week per person in year 3**
against two founders, where a rep manages 30–40.

A distributor with an existing sales team is available from launch.

## Decision

**A distributor covers golf clubs and bars from day 1.** We sell to them at
**R20.00 a tin**; they sell to the venue at R24.25, which retails at R45 — about
an **18% distributor margin**. DTC stays ours at R35.

## Why

- **It removes the coverage constraint entirely.** Their team has the feet; ours
  never could have.
- **It is far faster.** 120 golf clubs and 150 bars in year 1 against 60 and 40
  selling direct, and 300 clubs and 1,800 bars by year 5.
- **R20 is a thin cut.** At 18% they are taking less than the 26% a distributor
  would normally want, which is achievable because Get Lucky's access means we
  hand them warm outlets rather than asking them to prospect cold.
- **We stop paying for a field force we cannot afford.** No rep hires; the
  headcount becomes a trade marketing and key accounts manager to run the
  distributor relationship and keep the brand pulled through.

## What it costs

**R4.25 a tin against selling direct.** On the year-5 plan that is roughly R2.7m
of gross profit handed over — bought back in reach we could not otherwise have
had, and in a cost base without a sales force.

**We are one step removed from the sell-through data.** The four numbers the
whole business is being built to produce — tins per outlet per month, reorder
rate, sell-through by placement, cost to acquire an outlet — now sit in the
distributor's system rather than ours. **Contract for that data explicitly. A
licensee will want to see it, and "our distributor has it" is not an answer.**

**Marketing has to work harder.** A distributor pushes what already has pull.
Marketing runs at 10% of revenue here rather than 7%, because we are now buying
the demand that makes their sales team prioritise us over the hundreds of other
lines they carry.

## Consequences

- Net price on golf and bars: R24.25 → **R20.00**, rising 5% a year thereafter
- Debtor terms 30 → **45 days**; logistics move to bulk drops at their DC
- Field reps removed from the plan; trade marketing manager from month 31
- Year 5: **638,005 tins, R20.6m revenue, R9.5m EBITDA at 46%**
- Cumulative five-year EBITDA **R18.66m**, net profit **R13.02m**

## What else comes with it

**The distributor warehouses the stock.** One container, one delivery address,
one invoiced customer. No 3PL storage, no pick-and-pack, no courier to hundreds
of venues, no credit control across a long tail of small accounts, no route
accounting software. **Logistics fall from R1.35 a tin to R0.20 and overheads run
at 30% of a conventional base rather than 40%** — together worth about R790,000
of cumulative EBITDA and taking break-even from 4,500 tins a month to 3,750.

The counterpart is a **concentrated debtor book**: one customer at 45 days is now
most of the receivables, and our stock sits in their warehouse. Credit-check
before signing, consider insuring the balance, and agree in writing what happens
to stock on termination. Tracked in R-17.

## The cash problem it created, and how it was solved

Faster growth is bought with stock. The plan buys far more inventory in year 1,
and on the original year-2 trade finance assumption **month-12 cash fell to
R16,432** — effectively zero.

**Trade finance now draws from the third order, placed in month 8**
[SOURCE: founders]. By then the distributor has been selling since month 7 and
two shipments have landed and cleared, so there is a trading record and stock to
secure against.

| | Minimum cash |
| --- | ---: |
| R1m, facility from year 2 | R16,432 ⚠ |
| **R1m, facility from the third order (month 8)** | **R300,273** |
| R1.25m, facility from year 2 | R266,432 |

**The timing is worth more than R284,000 of equity and costs nothing.** The
condition is that a bank actually lends at that point — get an indicative term
sheet before committing to the R1m number. Tracked as R-18.

## Revisit when

- **The distributor underperforms on golf.** Golf is 300 outlets and the brand
  engine. If their team does not treat it as a priority, taking golf back
  in-house is affordable — it is a small universe and the founders are already
  there through Get Lucky.
- **Volumes justify renegotiating the 18%.** Distributor margins compress as a
  brand builds pull.

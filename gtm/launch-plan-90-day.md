# The first 90 days of selling

*Months 7–9 of the model — from first stock landing to the data that funds
everything after it.*

---

## The point of this

Not revenue. **Evidence.** Specifically: how many tins a golf club sells in a
month, and whether it buys more.

The entire five-year plan scales off that one number, and no external benchmark
for it exists anywhere in the world. We are going to find it out for about
R30,000 of stock and one person's time.

## Days 1–30 — place it

**Goal: 40 golf clubs stocked.**

Gauteng and Western Cape only. Do not spread. A rep who can be at any club within
90 minutes will out-sell a national footprint every time.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| The pitch | One display box. 12 tins. R291 ex-VAT. Sells at R45 each. **R178 of margin per box, 38%.** |
| The ask | Take one box. If it doesn't sell in six weeks, we take it back. |
| The hook | Free sampling stock for the halfway house and the starter |

**Placement priority: pro shop counter, next to the till.** Not on a shelf, not in
a basket. The counter or nothing.

**Measure from day one.** Every club: date placed, placement position, units
taken, date of first reorder. A spreadsheet is fine. Not measuring is not fine.

Launch event in week 3 — press, creators, club pros, and every tin photographed.

## Days 31–60 — learn from it

**Goal: first reorders, and 20 bars.**

- Revisit every one of the 40 clubs. In person. Count what's left in the box.
- **The reorder is the whole test.** A club that reorders has proved the product
  sells. A club that doesn't has told you something more valuable than a club
  that never stocked it.
- Start the bar channel: 20 outlets, metro, premium. Different buyer, different
  rhythm, and we need to know if it works at all before year 2 depends on it.
- Begin international creator seeding. Ship tins. No brief, no contract.

**The questions this month answers:**
- What's the actual monthly sell-through per club? (Modelled: 32)
- Does counter placement beat halfway house? By how much?
- What percentage reorder within 60 days?
- Do bars behave like golf clubs, or completely differently?

## Days 61–90 — decide on it

**Goal: 110 clubs, 140 bars — or an honest change of plan.**

By day 90 the numbers are in and they say one of three things:

| If sell-through is | Then |
| --- | --- |
| **Above ~32 tins/club/month** | The base case holds. Scale the outlet count, start the growth round conversation early, hold pricing. |
| **20–32** | The model works but slower. Push break-even out, raise a smaller growth round, lean harder on DTC and golf where margins are best. |
| **Below ~20** | **Stop and diagnose before spending more.** Is it price, placement, product strength, or the wrong channel? A 35g tin at R45 may simply be the wrong architecture — a smaller tin at R30 is a real alternative. Better to find this out on 40 clubs than on 300. |

Also due by day 90:
- [ ] Reorder rate by channel, written down
- [ ] Cost per new outlet acquired
- [ ] Second and third supplier quotes in hand
- [ ] **Third PO placed (month 9, per the model)** — the second went out in
      month 6, before launch, on forecast rather than on evidence
- [ ] Growth round narrative drafted off real data

## What would make this fail

- **Spreading too wide.** Forty clubs done properly beats 150 done at arm's length.
- **Not counting.** If we reach day 90 without sell-through per outlet per month,
  the 90 days were wasted regardless of revenue.
- **Discounting early.** A club that takes it at R45 tells us something. A club
  that takes it at R30 tells us nothing except that we panicked.
- **Adding a fourth flavour.** The answer to slow sell-through is never a new SKU.
- **Believing good news too fast.** Launch-month numbers are inflated by novelty
  and by us being in the room. Month 3 is the real number.

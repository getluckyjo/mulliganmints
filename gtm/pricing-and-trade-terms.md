## 1. Price architecture

| | |

| **Recommended retail price** | **R45.00 incl VAT** |
| Ex-VAT retail value | R39.13 |
| Pack | 35g tin |
| Effective price | R1.29 per gram |

### Why R45

| Benchmark | Price | Per gram |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Wilson's XXX (26g roll) | ~R12 | R0.46 |
| Mentos | R16–22 | ~R0.60 |
| **Fisherman's Friend (25g bag)** | **R28.99** | **R1.16** |
| **Mulligan Mints (35g tin)** | **R45.00** | **R1.29** |
| Altoids (50g tin, grey import) | R60–80 | R1.20–1.60 |

R45 sits above Fisherman's Friend and below imported Altoids, at a per-gram price
only 11% above Fisherman's Friend. **The premium is in the format and the brand,
not in the gram.** That is a defensible story in front of a buyer, and it leaves
room to hold price rather than discount.

**Do not launch below R45.** Coming down later is easy; going up is not. And in a
market where retail volume is flat and retail value is growing, price is the only
place growth lives.

### The display box — the unit that actually gets sold

Nobody buys "a tin" at trade. They buy a box.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| 12 tins per display box | |
| Club pays | **R291.00 ex-VAT** (R334.65 incl VAT) |
| Club sells for | **R540.00 incl VAT** (R469.57 ex-VAT) |
| **Club's gross margin** | **R178.57 per box — 38%** |

That is the pitch, and it fits on one line. Lead with the R178 a box.

*(Stated ex-VAT on both sides, which is how a VAT-registered club reads its own
margin. In pure cash terms the club pays out R334.65 and takes in R540 — but
R70.43 of that difference is output VAT it owes SARS, so quoting the cash gap as
margin overstates it. Do not do that in a sell sheet; buyers notice.)*

## 3. Rules

**Never discount the RSP to win a door.** A club that needs a discount to stock it
will not sell it. Give free sampling stock instead — a tin costs us R9.58 and
buys a trial; a R10 discount costs the same and buys nothing.

**One price for all direct venues.** Golf clubs and bars pay the same R24.25. The
moment we have two prices in one market we have a negotiation on every call.

**Volume rewards, not price cuts.** Free stock on a 10-box order, not a lower unit
price. It protects the RSP and puts more product in hands.

**Annual increase of ~5%, every year, on a fixed date.** Announced in advance.
Predictable increases are accepted; surprise increases are argued about. This
matters more than it sounds — we buy in dollars and sell in rand, so if we do not
increase annually the rand does the pricing for us.

**Retail entry: no listing without a promotional plan.** A listing without an
agreed promo grid and a review date is how a brand pays a listing fee and then
gets delisted at the next range review.

## 4. Corporate and gifting — the underpriced opportunity

Corporate golf days, club championships, member gifts and conference bags.

- Higher volumes, single orders, paid up front, near-zero cost to serve
- Price at **R28–32 ex-VAT** for 500+ tins
- Custom sleeve or a co-branded tin at higher volumes — a genuine premium
- No shelf, no rep visit, no returns

This is not modelled anywhere in the plan. It is a real revenue line, it suits
the product exactly, and it is upside.

## 5. Payment terms — and the reason to be strict

The model assumes 30 days for direct venues and 65 for retail. **We pay our
supplier 30% at order and 70% at bill of lading, four months before the stock
sells.** Every extra day of debtor terms is a day of our own cash funding somebody
else's inventory.

Practical position:
- Direct venues: 30 days, strictly. First order cash or card.
- New venues: first order paid on delivery. No exceptions in year 1.
- Retail: their terms are non-negotiable. Price for it — that is what the R21.00
  net already reflects.
- Export: letter of credit or payment before shipment until a relationship is
  three orders old.

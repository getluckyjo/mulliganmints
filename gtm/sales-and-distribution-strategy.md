# Sales and distribution strategy

*For the dataroom. How Mulligan Mints physically gets from a container in Durban
onto a pro shop counter, and who we have to convince along the way.*

---

## The question we set out to answer

**Do South African golf clubs order through liquor distributors?**

**No — and that matters, because it is the obvious assumption and it is wrong.**

South African liquor distribution is beverage-only and vertically controlled.
Heineken Beverages (Distell, Namibia Breweries and Heineken SA combined in 2023),
AB InBev/SAB, Diageo and Pernod Ricard run their own routes; redistributors like
Super Discount Liquors and Oasis carry those same principals' lines. **Their reps
sell beer, wine and spirits.** A confectionery SKU is not their category, not
their margin, and not what their rep is incentivised on. A mint does not travel
on a beer truck.

Confectionery reaches on-consumption venues through **FMCG distributors** —
Key Distributors (Nestlé, Unilever, Tiger Brands, Reckitt, GSK portfolios; depots
in Cape Town, Johannesburg, Durban, Nelspruit, Polokwane and Bloemfontein, on
48-hour delivery) and Maspark (snacks, confectionery and beverages from a Cape
Town facility into retailers, wholesalers, forecourts and independents) — or
through **cash and carry**, where a great many venues simply send someone to
Makro or a Massmart CBW/Jumbo/Trident branch each week.

## The finding that shapes everything: a golf club is not one customer

This is the single most important thing in this document.

**At most South African clubs the pro shop is not run by the club.** It is leased
or franchised:

- **Clovelly Golf Club** — pro shop operated by **Global Golf**, a national company
- **Wanderers Golf Club** — a PGA of South Africa affiliated shop run by
  professionals
- **Port Shepstone Country Club** — run by the resident PGA professional, who is
  also the course superintendent

So one golf club can contain **three separate buying decisions**:

| Who | Controls | What they care about |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The pro shop operator** — often a PGA pro or a company like Global Golf, trading on their own account | The counter. The highest-value placement we have. | Margin per square centimetre of counter, and cash tied up in stock |
| **The F&B or bar operator** — frequently a contracted caterer, not the club | The bar, the halfway house, the beverage cart | Speed of sale, no waste, simple reordering |
| **The club manager / GM** | Corporate golf days, member gifts, club championship stock | Member experience, sponsorship, doing the club a favour |

**"Sign a golf club" is not one sale.** The pitch, the price and the person are
different for each. A rep who walks into the clubhouse and asks for "the person
who buys the sweets" gets three shrugs.

**Practical consequence:** the first question on every call is *"who owns the pro
shop — the club or you?"* The answer determines everything that follows. This
belongs in the call sheet from day one.

## Route to market by channel

| Channel | Who we sell to | How they order | Our route |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Golf pro shop** | The shop operator (leased pro or company) | Direct from brands and golf distributors; they already buy balls, gloves and tees this way | **Direct.** They are used to buying from brands. |
| **Halfway house / bar** | The F&B operator or contracted caterer | Liquor from beverage distributors; food and snacks from cash and carry or an FMCG distributor | **Direct**, then a distributor for the tail |
| **Corporate golf days** | Event organiser or club GM | Ad hoc, project-based, paid up front | **Direct.** Highest margin, zero cost to serve. |
| **Bars and restaurants** | Owner or manager | Liquor on beverage routes; snacks from cash and carry or FMCG distributor | **Direct** in metros, **distributor** beyond |
| **DTC** | The golfer | Our webshop | **Direct** |
| **Retail** | Category buyer | Listing, planogram, 65-day terms | **Out of the plan** ([0008](../docs/decisions/0008-bootstrap-route.md)) |

## Our model: direct first, and why

**Direct is not ideology — it is arithmetic.** At R24.25 direct against R18.00
through a distributor, every tin sold direct is **R6.25 of extra margin**. On the
year-3 plan that difference is about R900,000. We do not have R900,000 to give
away for reach we can cover ourselves.

Direct also gives us the four numbers the whole business is being built to
produce: sell-through per outlet per month, reorder rate, sell-through by
placement, and cost to acquire an outlet. **A distributor sells our tins and
keeps that data.** Until we know those numbers, we cannot afford to be one step
removed from them.

### The Get Lucky advantage, stated precisely

Get Lucky Golf Club already has access to **30 golf courses** and runs **600+
promotions a year** across them. Two things follow:

1. **Warm access to the first 30 clubs**, with an existing relationship and a
   reason to be on site. That is why the plan exits year 1 at 60 clubs with no
   sales hire.
2. **A sampling engine that is already paid for.** Roughly fifty promotions a
   month put tins into golfers' hands at R10.29 a tin — cheaper and better
   converting than any advertising we could buy.

**Both need to be put on a written footing.** A plan that rests on another
company's relationships should not rest on goodwill alone. Tracked as R-13 in
`ops/risk-register.md`.

## The coverage problem, and how we solve it

A field rep realistically manages **30–40 calls a week**. Here is what the plan
actually demands, on a four-week call cycle:

| Year | Golf | Bars | Outlets | Field heads | Outlets/head | Calls/week each |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 60 | 40 | 100 | 2 | 50 | 13 |
| 2 | 120 | 120 | 240 | 2 | 120 | 30 |
| **3** | **180** | **240** | **420** | **2** | **210** | **53** ⚠ |
| 4 | 230 | 360 | 590 | 3 | 197 | 49 ⚠ |
| 5 | 270 | 480 | 750 | 4 | 188 | 47 ⚠ |

**From year 3 the plan asks for more calls than people can physically make.**
Years 1 and 2 are comfortable. Year 3 is not, and it does not resolve itself by
hiring — even at four field heads in year 5 we are at 47 calls a week each.

Three ways out, and we should do all three:

**1. Tier the outlets.** The top 20% of accounts generate most of the volume and
deserve a monthly visit. The tail does not. A tiered cycle — monthly for A
accounts, quarterly for C accounts — takes year 3 from 53 calls a week to roughly
30, which is achievable with the two founders.

**2. Make reordering low-touch.** A WhatsApp reorder line and a simple online
account mean an outlet can restock without a visit. This is cheap to build, it is
what the tail actually wants, and it is the single highest-leverage thing we can
do for field productivity. **Build it before year 2, not during year 3.**

**3. Hand the bar tail to a distributor from year 3.** This is the honest use for
Key Distributors or Maspark: not the golf clubs, which we keep forever, and not
our best bar accounts — the long tail of small outlets that cost more to visit
than they return. We give up R6.25 a tin on that tail and buy back the field time.

> **This is a real gap in the current model.** The financial plan assumes no
> distributor at all and one rep from month 30. Either the reorder system carries
> the load, or a distributor takes the bar tail and year-3 margin drops. The
> model can be re-run either way — worth deciding before the dataroom goes out,
> because a sharp investor will do this arithmetic.

## Trade terms by route

| Route | Our net per tin | Their margin | Terms |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Golf pro shop, direct | R24.25 | 38% on R45 RSP | 30 days; first order on delivery |
| Bar, direct | R24.25 | 38% | 30 days; first order on delivery |
| Corporate / gifting, 500+ tins | R28–32 | n/a | Paid up front |
| Distributor (bar tail, from yr 3) | R18.00 | ~26% to them | 45 days |
| DTC | R35.00 | n/a | Paid up front |

**One price for all direct venues.** The moment there are two prices in one
market, there is a negotiation on every call.

**The unit that gets sold is the display box, not the tin.** Twelve tins for
R291 ex-VAT, retailing at R540 incl VAT — **R178.57 of margin, 38%**. Lead with
that.

## How we actually win a club

1. **Establish who owns the pro shop.** Club or concession. Everything follows.
2. **Sample first, sell second.** Hand over tins. The product does the work —
   it is genuinely strong and people react to it.
3. **Place one display box on the counter.** Not a shelf, not a basket. The
   counter, next to the till, where people pay for their round.
4. **Free sampling stock for the halfway house and the starter.** This is
   marketing spend, not shrinkage, and it should be budgeted as such.
5. **Return in three weeks and count what is left.** The reorder is the entire
   test. A club that reorders has proved the product sells.
6. **Then ask for the corporate golf day.** Once the counter is working, the
   goodie-bag order is a much easier conversation — and it is the most profitable
   order in the business.

## What we still do not know

Stated plainly, because a dataroom that hides its gaps is worse than one that
names them:

1. **What share of SA clubs lease their pro shop versus running it themselves.**
   We have three worked examples, not a survey. This changes who we call and how
   the pitch is framed, and thirty phone calls would answer it.
2. **Whether any distributor treats golf clubs as a defined channel.** We found
   none. If one exists, it would be a significant shortcut.
3. **Whether golf club groups buy centrally.** Sun International runs five
   courses; if group-level buying exists anywhere, one conversation replaces
   fifty.
4. **What a pro shop's actual counter sell-through is** for any impulse item.
   No benchmark exists. The 90-day pilot is the only way to find out.

**Items 1 to 3 are answerable this month, by telephone, for nothing.** They
should be closed before the dataroom goes out.

# IP and trademark strategy

> **The brand is the asset.** In year 4 of the plan, licensing revenue arrives at
> roughly 95% margin and consumes no working capital. That revenue exists only if
> the trademark portfolio exists. Everything in this document is therefore a
> capital investment, not a legal cost.

---

## 1. File first, publish second

**File the South African trademark before any artwork is shown publicly** —
before the launch event, before the first Instagram post, before the webshop
goes live.

South Africa is a first-to-file jurisdiction. Publishing first and filing later
invites exactly the problem we cannot afford: someone else holding our name in a
market we intend to license.

Budgeted at **R28,000** in the setup costs, month 1.

## 2. South African filings

| Class | Covers | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **30** | Confectionery, mints, sweets — **the core** | **Essential** |
| **25** | Clothing, headgear — caps, shirts, merchandise | High |
| **35** | Retail and online retail services | Medium |
| **28** | Sporting goods — ball markers, tee accessories | Medium |
| **32/33** | Beverages | Optional, defensive |

File **30 and 25 at minimum**. Class 25 matters more than it looks: golf brands
sell apparel, and a licensee will want it.

**What to file:** the word mark `MULLIGAN MINTS`, the word mark `MULLIGAN` in
class 30 if it is available, the logo/device mark once PJ Offner delivers it, and
the tagline `LIKE IT NEVER HAPPENED` — taglines are registrable and this one is
distinctive enough to be worth it.

## 3. Clearance searches — before spending on the brand

Run these before PJ Offner starts, not after:

- [ ] CIPC trademark database, South Africa
- [ ] WIPO Global Brand Database
- [ ] EUIPO and UKIPO — our first export markets
- [ ] USPTO — the biggest prize and the most crowded register
- [ ] Domain availability: `mulliganmints.com`, `.co.za`, `.ie`
- [ ] Social handles across Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, X
- [ ] Plain search for existing use of "Mulligan" in food and drink

**"Mulligan" is a common Irish surname and a widely used golf term.** Expect prior
registrations in some classes and territories. Better to find them now than after
the brand development — which under the term sheet costs 10% of the company.

## 4. International — Madrid Protocol

South Africa is not a member of the Madrid Protocol, so we cannot use a South
African filing as the basing application. Two practical routes:

1. **Direct national filings** in the priority markets, or
2. **Base a Madrid application on a filing in a member state** (an EU or UK
   filing, for example) and designate from there.

Worth taking advice on the cheaper structure before filing anything outside SA.

### Priority markets and timing

| When | Markets | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Month 1** | South Africa | Home market, first to file |
| **Within 6 months** | UK, EU, USA | The 6-month Paris Convention priority window lets these claim the SA filing date. **Do not miss this window** — it is free priority. |
| **Year 2** | Australia, UAE | Tier 1 export targets |
| **Year 3–4** | Per licensee | A licensee will require registration in their territory before signing |

**The six-month priority window is the single most time-sensitive item in this
document.** Budget roughly R120,000 for the UK/EU/US filings and treat it as
non-negotiable.

## 5. Other IP

| Asset | Protection | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brand identity, illustration, guidelines | Copyright | **Take full assignment from PJ Offner in writing.** Commissioned work does not automatically vest in the commissioner. This must be in the engagement letter, not agreed afterwards. |
| Tin design | Registered design (aesthetic) | Worth considering if the tin has a distinctive shape or emboss |
| Formula | Trade secret | Do not attempt to patent a mint formula. Keep the specification confidential and put an NDA in place with every supplier quoted. |
| Domains and handles | Registration | Secure everything on the day the name is decided |
| Photography and content | Copyright | Assignment or full commercial licence from every photographer and creator |

## 6. Licensing readiness — what year 4 requires

A licensee will not sign without:

1. Registered trademarks **in their territory**, in the relevant classes
2. Clean title — no encumbrances, no prior licences, no disputes
3. A brand book defining what they may and may not do
4. A product specification they can manufacture to
5. Quality control and audit rights written into the agreement
6. Documented evidence of use — because unused marks become vulnerable to
   non-use cancellation

**Non-use is a real risk.** In most jurisdictions a mark unused for three to five
years can be cancelled. If we register in the US in year 1 and do not trade there
until year 5, the registration may be vulnerable. Take advice on intent-to-use
filings and on maintaining registrations in markets we are not yet trading in.

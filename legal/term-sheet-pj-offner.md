# Term sheet — brand equity partnership

**Mulligan Mints (Pty) Ltd** *(in formation)* **and PJ Offner**

**Date:** ______________ 2026

---

## In plain terms

PJ builds the Mulligan Mints brand — the identity, the packaging artwork and the
brand guidelines. In return he gets **10% of the company**, not a fee.

He becomes a shareholder on day one. The shares vest as the work is delivered, so
both sides carry the risk together. The company owns everything he makes,
outright and forever, because that ownership is what the whole business is built
on.

That's the deal. The rest of this document is the detail.

## 3. What PJ delivers

The scope is the creative brief at `brand/brand-brief-pj-offner.md`, which is
attached to this term sheet and forms part of it. In summary:

**Core identity** — primary wordmark and secondary lockup; a brand mark that
works standalone at 8mm; colour palette including a flavour-coding system for
three SKUs with room for two more; typography; illustration style with one fully
worked hero illustration plus supporting assets.

**Packaging** — tin artwork (lid, base, sides) for three flavours to the
supplier's dieline; display box artwork; shipper carton markings.

**Applications** — pro-shop counter display and sampling kit; social templates;
merchandise starters (cap, tee bag, ball marker, towel); digital logo suite and
favicon.

**The brand book** — full guidelines covering identity, tone of voice, do's and
don'ts, and the brand story.

**Files** — all working files (`.ai`, `.psd`, `.indd`) plus print-ready and
screen exports of every deliverable.

### Two constraints that are not negotiable

**Origin.** The product is manufactured in China and the Company is South
African. The Irish design reference must work as character and homage — never as
a claim of provenance. No "Product of Ireland", no "Irish recipe", no
shamrock-and-Gaelic lockup that functions as a country-of-origin mark. The reason
is set out in `research/04-global-opportunity.md` §6.

**Regulatory space.** The tin must carry ingredients, a nutritional table,
sweetener declarations, allergens, net weight, country of origin, importer
details, batch code and best-before date — and must leave physical room for a
front-of-pack warning logo under draft regulation R3337, whether or not one is
required at launch.

## 5. Vesting

PJ's shares are **issued in full on signature**, so he is a shareholder from day
one with a shareholder's rights. They are subject to a **repurchase right**: the
Company may buy back any unvested shares for **R1 in total** if the relationship
ends before the work is finished.

The repurchase right falls away in four tranches:

| Tranche | Vests on | Portion |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| 1 | Signature of this term sheet and the definitive documents | **20%** |
| 2 | Written acceptance by the Company of the final core identity | **25%** |
| 3 | Delivery of production-ready packaging artwork, accepted by the Company **and confirmed as printable by the tin supplier** | **30%** |
| 4 | Delivery of the brand book, all working files, and signed IP assignment | **25%** |

Acceptance will not be unreasonably withheld or delayed. If the Company does not
respond in writing within **10 business days** of a tranche being submitted, that
tranche is treated as accepted.

**Accelerated vesting.** All unvested shares vest immediately on a sale of the
Company, or if the Company terminates the engagement other than for PJ's material
breach.

## 6. Intellectual property — the clause that matters most

**All intellectual property in the Deliverables vests in the Company, absolutely
and worldwide, in perpetuity.** That includes copyright, design rights, trade
mark rights and all working files, in every medium, for every purpose, including
purposes not yet contemplated.

PJ signs a **separate written IP assignment** in favour of the Company on
delivery of each tranche, and a final consolidated assignment on completion.
Signing the consolidated assignment is a **condition of tranche 4 vesting**.

South African law does not automatically transfer copyright in commissioned work
of this kind to the person who commissioned it. That is why this is written down
rather than assumed.

**Why it is non-negotiable, said openly:** the Company's five-year plan turns on
licensing the brand into other territories from year 4 onward. A licensee's
lawyers will examine the chain of title before signing anything. If the Company
cannot show it owns the brand outright, there is no licensing business — and PJ's
10% is worth considerably less as a result. Clean ownership is in both parties'
interest.

### PJ warrants that

1. The Deliverables are his original work and do not infringe anyone else's
   rights.
2. **Every typeface, image, illustration and asset used is licensed for
   commercial use, for packaging and unlimited print runs, and that licence is
   transferable to the Company** — at PJ's cost. Font licensing for packaging is
   a genuine trap and this is where it gets caught.
3. He will **disclose in writing any material generated using AI tools**, and
   where such material forms part of a deliverable, will work with the Company to
   ensure the Company's ownership is not compromised. This is a disclosure
   requirement, not a prohibition.
4. He has not granted anyone else rights in the Deliverables.

### What PJ keeps

**Attribution.** PJ is credited as the designer of the Mulligan Mints brand in
the brand book, on the Company's website, and in press and award submissions
where a design credit is customary.

**Portfolio rights.** PJ may show the work in his portfolio, on his website, on
social media and in award entries — from the date of the Company's public launch,
or 12 months after delivery if the Company has not launched by then.

## 7. Dilution — stated plainly so nobody is surprised later

PJ's 10% is **10% of the Company as it stands on the day of issue.** It is not
protected against future dilution. When the Company issues new shares — to
investors, to an employee option pool, to anyone — PJ is diluted in the same
proportion as the Founder.

Illustratively, on the Company's current funding plan:

| After a 10% option pool | 81.0% | **9.0%** | 10.0% | — |

**PJ has pre-emptive rights**: if the Company issues new shares, PJ may subscribe
for enough of them to maintain his percentage, on the same terms as everyone
else. He is under no obligation to.

## 9. Future work

This term sheet covers the Deliverables in clause 3. **It does not cover
everything the Company ever needs designed.**

Further work — artwork for new flavours or formats, campaign design, a packaging
refresh, retail or trade collateral, work for a licensee — is a **separate,
paid engagement**, on terms agreed at the time.

**Right of first refusal.** For **three years** from completion, the Company will
offer PJ first refusal on brand design work for Mulligan Mints, on commercial
terms and a timeline reasonably comparable to what it could obtain elsewhere. If
PJ declines or cannot meet the timeline, the Company is free to go elsewhere.

## 10. Restraint

For as long as PJ holds shares, and for **24 months** afterwards, PJ will not
create brand identity or packaging design for a **pressed mint, breath mint or
mint confectionery brand** that competes with the Company in a market where the
Company trades or has a licensee.

This is deliberately narrow. It does not restrict PJ's work in confectionery
generally, in food and beverage, in golf, or anywhere else.

## 11. Confidentiality

Each party keeps the other's confidential information confidential, during the
engagement and for three years afterwards. That includes the Company's financial
model, supplier terms, pricing and commercial plans, and PJ's unpublished work
and process.

Clause 6's portfolio rights are a carve-out from this — once launch has happened,
PJ may show the work.

## 12. Tax

Shares issued in return for services are a taxable event. Under **section 8C of
the Income Tax Act**, shares acquired by virtue of services and subject to
restrictions are generally taxed as ordinary income when the restrictions lift —
not as a capital gain, and not necessarily when there is any cash to pay the tax
with.

**Each party takes its own tax advice before signing.** Neither party is advising
the other on tax, and neither is responsible for the other's tax. This is flagged
here because it is a real and commonly missed consequence, not because either
side has taken a view on it.

explicitly unverified. **The shares may end up worth nothing.** PJ enters this
agreement on that basis, with his eyes open.

# Market analysis — strong mints, South Africa and beyond

*Compiled August 2026. Every external figure is cited in [`sources.md`](sources.md).*

---

## 1. The category we are entering

Mulligan Mints does not compete in "mints". It competes in a much narrower and
much more defensible sub-category: **the strong mint bought as a ritual, not as
a breath fix.**

That distinction runs through the whole plan. A Tic Tac is a habit. A Mentos is
an impulse. A Fisherman's Friend is an *event* — people remember the first one
they ate, they warn their friends, and they buy the same flavour for thirty years.
Strong mints are the only part of the confectionery aisle where the product
experience is strong enough to carry a brand story on its own.

## 2. Market size

### Global

| Measure | Figure | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Global mints market, 2025 | USD 7.19bn | Market Research Future |
| Forecast 2035 | USD 12.71bn @ 5.9% CAGR | Market Research Future |
| Global breath mints, 2025 | USD 4.2bn | Dataintelo |
| Category leadership | Wrigley 18.4%, Perfetti Van Melle 12.7% | Ken Research |

Estimates vary widely by scope definition (USD 4.2bn to USD 7.2bn for what is
nominally the same category), which is itself the useful signal: **this is a
category nobody measures precisely, because it is fragmented and the big players
treat it as a line extension rather than a business.** That is the gap a focused
brand walks into.

### South Africa

| Measure | Figure | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SA confectionery market, 2025 | USD 1.95bn (~R31bn) | Mordor Intelligence |
| Forecast 2030 | USD 2.66bn @ 6.44% CAGR | Mordor Intelligence |
| Position in Africa | Largest confectionery market on the continent | Mordor Intelligence |
| Volume vs value | Retail **value** growing; retail **volume** stagnant | Euromonitor |

That last line matters more than the headline. South African confectionery is
growing in rand and flat in tonnage. Consumers are not eating more sweets; they
are paying more per sweet. **Every point of growth available in this market is a
premiumisation point.** A R45 tin in a category where the incumbent sells a R12
roll is not fighting the trend — it *is* the trend.

**Our estimate of the addressable SA mint category: R1.2–1.5bn at retail**
(4–5% of total confectionery, consistent with how mints size in comparable
markets). Year 5 of our base case puts 1.14m tins into South Africa, which at
R45 RSP is **R51m of retail value — roughly 3.5–4% of that category.**

That is the number to argue about. It is ambitious for a five-year-old
challenger, and it is not absurd: it assumes we own a niche the incumbents are
not defending, not that we take share from Mentos.

## 3. Why this category, why now

**1. Sugar-free is now the default expectation, not a premium claim.**
Global demand is being pushed toward lower-sugar formats by both consumers and
regulators. Our product is sugar-free from day one, which costs us nothing in
positioning and pre-empts the front-of-pack labelling regime coming in SA (draft
R3337 — see `legal/compliance-checklist-sa.md`). Note the catch: R3337 also
proposes a warning logo for *artificial sweeteners*. Sweetener choice is
therefore a regulatory decision, not just a taste one.

**2. The tin is the product.** In a category where everything else is a
disposable roll or a plastic flip-pack, a tin is a keepsake. It sits on a
dashboard, in a golf bag, on a bar counter. It gets refilled, reused and
photographed. Altoids built a 25%+ US share on essentially this insight plus
low-cost, edgy media — no mass advertising, just a tin and a tone of voice.

**3. South African confectionery is dominated by two multinationals who do not
defend niches.** Mondelēz (Beacon, Wilson's XXX, Halls) and Perfetti Van Melle
(Mentos, Smint) run the category. Neither has a premium strong-mint tin in
market. A brand that is too small for them to care about for three years is a
brand that gets three years to build.

**4. Golf content is a genuine global media channel now, not a niche.**
Good Good Golf raised USD 45m in March 2025 and did over USD 40m in revenue.
Bob Does Sports has 1.1m subscribers and has already launched its own apparel
line and its own alcoholic drink brand. The audience for "golf as culture"
buys products from the people they watch. This is the single biggest asymmetry
in the plan: **a South African mint brand can be built on a global media
channel at South African cost.**

## 4. Consumer and occasion

The product is bought for a moment, and there are three of them:

| Occasion | Where | What the mint does | Why they pay R45 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The round** | Golf club pro shop, halfway house, cart | Punctuates the round; the thing you hand your fourball on the tee | It is part of the kit, priced like a sleeve of balls |
| **The bar** | Pub, restaurant, on the counter at the till | Resets you before the next thing | Impulse at a price nobody thinks about |
| **The gift / the keepsake** | Pro shop, online, corporate golf days | It is a tin with a joke on it | It is a R45 present that lands |

The tagline — *Like it never happened* — is doing the work in all three. It is a
golf joke first: the mulligan, the shot you take again, the one that never
counted.

> **A framing decision that needs making early.** In a bar, "like it never
> happened" reads differently — as *hide what you have been drinking*. That
> reading is funny, it is on-brand, and it is a liability. It is the kind of
> thing that gets a product declined by a Clicks or Dis-Chem buyer on
> responsible-marketing grounds, and it is exactly what a global licensee's brand
> safety review will flag. **Keep the joke anchored in golf.** The bad shot, the
> missed putt, the thing that never counted — never the drink. This costs us
> nothing creatively and removes a real commercial risk. Carried in
> `ops/risk-register.md` as R-07.

## 5. What the market data does not tell us

Being explicit about the gaps, because they are the ones that matter:

- **No SA-specific mint category size exists publicly.** Our R1.2–1.5bn estimate
  is derived, not sourced. A Euromonitor *Sugar Confectionery in South Africa*
  report would settle it and is worth buying before the growth round.
- **No SA-specific mint brand share data.** We know who the players are; we do
  not know their share.
- **No published count of licensed on-consumption outlets in SA.** Stats SA's
  food-and-beverage sampling frame of 5,178 formal enterprises is the best
  public proxy, and it undercounts bars badly.
- **Nobody publishes golf-course retail sell-through.** The single most important
  number in our model — tins per club per month — has no external benchmark.
  It has to be measured, in our own venues, in the first 90 days.

The correct response to all four is the same: **the pilot is the research.**
See `gtm/launch-plan-90-day.md`.

The top-right quadrant in South Africa is empty. Altoids is available only as a
grey-market import at R60–80. Nobody is building a local premium strong mint.

## 2. The brand we are modelled on — Fisherman's Friend

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Owner | Lofthouse of Fleetwood Ltd (family-owned, UK) |
| Origin | Fleetwood, 1865 — made for Icelandic deep-sea fishermen |
| Volume | **5 billion lozenges a year**, ~97% exported |
| Revenue | £55.9m (2018), £56.7m (2019), £33.1m (2021) |
| SA price | **R28.99 / 25g** at Dis-Chem, across the full flavour range |
| SA availability | Dis-Chem, Clicks, Checkers — sits in *both* the sweets aisle and the sore-throat aisle |

**What we take from it.** A single-product, family-owned business built a
£50m+ global brand on one uncompromising sensory promise and near-zero
advertising. Its distribution model is almost entirely export-through-importers
— which is the template for our own global route. Its shelf price in South Africa
(R28.99 for 25g in a paper bag) sets the ceiling we have to justify a premium
against.

**What we do differently.** Fisherman's Friend is a medicinal product that
became a sweet. It lives next to the throat lozenges. It has no occasion, no
culture and no community — it is bought, not chosen. Mulligan Mints is a mint
with a *place*: a sport, a joke, a moment. That is the whole differentiation.

Note also the revenue line: £56.7m in 2019, £33.1m in 2021. A single-SKU global
brand is exposed. Diversification of format and channel is not a nice-to-have.

## 3. The brand we are modelled on — Altoids

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Owner | Mars Wrigley |
| Origin | Smith & Co., London, 1780 — originally a stomach remedy |
| Positioning | *"The Original Celebrated Curiously Strong Mints"* since 1995 |
| The lesson | Leo Burnett took Altoids from near-zero to **25%+ US share** on low-cost, edgy, out-of-home media — buses, bar postcards, concert tickets, a tugboat |

**What we take from it.** Three things, precisely:

1. **The tin is the brand asset**, not the packaging. It is the thing people keep.
2. **A strength claim owned in language beats a strength claim owned in formula.**
   "Curiously strong" is not a spec. It is a promise you cannot copy.
3. **Category share was won on media placement, not media budget.** Altoids was
   built in the places its audience already was. For us that is golf content,
   golf clubs, and bars — not television.

### The Wilson's XXX problem, stated honestly

Wilson's XXX is the real competitor, and it is owned by Mondelēz. It already has:
extra-strong credentials, South African heritage, national distribution, and a
price a third of ours.

We do not beat it on any of those. We beat it on the three things it does not have:

- **An occasion.** Wilson's is a mint. Mulligan is the mint you eat on the tee.
- **A tin.** Wilson's is a roll in a pocket. Ours is an object on a counter.
- **A community.** Wilson's has nostalgia. Nostalgia is not a community — it does
  not make content, wear the merch or bring its fourball along.

If Mulligan Mints becomes "a nicer Wilson's XXX", we lose. If it becomes "the
mint golf drinks with", Wilson's is irrelevant to us and we to it.

## 5. Adjacent proof — what golf-culture brands are actually doing

This is the most important competitive intelligence in the document, because it
is where the model comes from.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Bob Does Sports** | 1.1m YouTube subscribers. Founded 2021. Callaway sponsorship. Has launched **Breezy** (apparel) and **Have A Day** (an alcoholic drink brand). |
| **Good Good Golf** | 3m+ subscribers across channels, 15 creators. **USD 40m+ revenue** in the last reported year. Raised **USD 45m** in March 2025 (Creator Sports Capital, with Peyton Manning's Omaha Productions). Co-branded Callaway gear in Dick's Sporting Goods. |

Read those two rows again. **Golf creators are not selling ad slots any more —
they are launching consumer products, and those products are working.** A drinks
brand and an apparel brand have both come out of a channel with roughly one
million subscribers.

That is simultaneously our biggest opportunity and our biggest competitive risk:

- **Opportunity:** a well-made mint tin is a natural fit for exactly this
  ecosystem, and no one has done it. We can be first.
- **Risk:** any of these channels could launch a mint tomorrow, with an audience
  we would need five years and R20m to build. **Speed and a defensible brand
  matter more than perfect product.**

The strategic implication is in `research/04-global-opportunity.md`: we should
treat US golf creators as a *partnership and licensing route*, not as
competition to be avoided.

## 6. Where we win, in one paragraph

The strong-mint category is old, boring, unbranded and owned by companies that do
not defend it. The premium tin format is empty in South Africa. The golf-culture
audience is proven to buy products from brands it feels ownership of, globally,
and is being served by nobody in this category. Mulligan Mints is a plausible
brand precisely because it is a *small* idea executed at a *specific* moment —
not a better mint, but the mint that belongs somewhere.
